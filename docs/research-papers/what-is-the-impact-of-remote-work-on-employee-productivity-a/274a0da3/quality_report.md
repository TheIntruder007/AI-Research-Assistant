## Missing citations
- (What is the impact of remote work on employee productivity and well-being?) A central tension in the existing evidence lies between the potential for increased flexibility and autonomy versus the risks of role conflict and isolation.
- (What is the impact of remote work on employee productivity and well-being?) Ultimately, the net impact depends on whether organizational structures can mitigate challenges like work-home interference while leveraging benefits such as autonomy and reduced commuting time.
- (The 'Concordance' Gap: Resolving Task-Family Discordance in Parental Remote Work) The literature currently offers no validated frameworks or software extensions to achieve the theorized 'concordance' where children are incorporated into parental work activities (analogous to historical family farming).
- (The 'Concordance' Gap: Resolving Task-Family Discordance in Parental Remote Work) The authors theorize that concordance can be achieved via 'designing imaginative extensions to the parent's professional work software,' but no such tools or studies exist in the corpus.
- (The Measurement Standardization Gap in Teacher Well-Being Research) The review reports wide-ranging prevalence figures (e.g., burnout 25.12% to 74%) which are likely incomparable due to these methodological differences.
- (The Intervention Efficacy Gap for Educators) No studies have evaluated the development, implementation, monitoring, or evaluation of these specific programs to determine if they actually reduce burnout or improve mental health outcomes.
- (The Longitudinal Sustainability Gap) Most studies capture the initial shock of the pandemic (2020-2021) but lack longitudinal data on the sustainability of WFH arrangements.
- (Conclusion) Uncertainty persists because many studies rely on cross-sectional designs or self-reported metrics that may not capture long-term productivity trends or nuanced well-being changes.

## Known limitations
- This analysis is constrained by the small corpus of six papers, which limits the breadth of populations covered (focusing heavily on teachers, healthcare workers, and general US employees). The verification searches for author-flagged candidates returned no additional papers, suggesting these gaps may be genuinely open or that the specific search terms used in the verification phase were too narrow. The reliance on abstracts and metadata means full methodological details (e.g., exact sample sizes, specific scales used) are inferred rather than fully verified.
